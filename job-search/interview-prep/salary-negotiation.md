## Anchoring Strategy

**Never give a number first.** If pushed:

> "I'm focused on finding the right fit — compensation should reflect the scope and impact of the role. What's the budget you've set for this position?"

If they push harder:

> "Based on my research for senior design roles at Series A–B companies, I'm targeting the ₹40–55L range for base, with equity reflecting the founding team risk I'd be taking on."

Always anchor 15–20% above your actual floor. They'll negotiate down — let them.

---

## Counter-Offer Script

After initial offer:

> "Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about [company]. The offer is a starting point. Based on [comparable roles / scope of work / equity stage risk], I was expecting something closer to [target]. Is there flexibility there?"

If they say no:

> "I understand. Can we discuss the equity component instead? Given I'd be joining at [stage] with [risk factors], I'd expect [target % or cliff/vesting terms]."

If they hold firm on both:

> "What else is on the table — signing bonus, learning budget, review cycle timing?"

---

## Equity Evaluation Checklist

Before saying yes to any startup:

1. What % of the company does your grant represent?
2. What's the current valuation / last round price per share?
3. What's the vesting schedule and cliff? (standard: 4yr / 1yr cliff)
4. What happens to unvested equity if you're let go?
5. What's the preference stack? (liquidation preference — matters at exit)
6. Exercise window after leaving? (10yr window is now table stakes, 90 days is a trap)
7. Has any equity been repriced or restructured?

---

## Competing Offer Leverage

If you have two offers:

> "I have another offer I'm evaluating. I'd prefer to join [company] because [specific reason tied to their mission]. If you can match [number], I can give you a decision by [date]."

Don't bluff. Only use if you have a real offer.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Accepting verbally before reading the written offer
- Not asking about vesting cliff upfront
- Negotiating on comp before the company is sold on you (earn conviction first)
- Accepting the first number out of desperation (especially with 3-month pressure — resist)
- Forgetting to negotiate non-cash: remote equipment budget, conference allowance, flexible hours
