03 — Design Decisions

Five decisions that defined the spatial UX.

01

Gaze+pinch vs controller

We chose Gaze+pinch Over Controllers

Eye-tracking precision for natural selection allows for a reduced barrier between thought and action. By eliminating physical peripherals, we enable a more visceral connection to the digital environment, leveraging innate human motor skills.

02

Voice AI vs menus

We chose Voice AI Over Hierarchical Menus

Intent-driven interaction reduces cognitive load by removing the need to navigate complex directory structures. Users simply speak their objective, and Orbit's semantic engine manifests the required interface contextually.

03

Depth planes vs flat hierarchy

We chose Depth planes Over Flat 2D Layouts

Z-axis separation for clearer spatial focus allows users to stack information chronologically and contextually. This mimics physical desk space, utilizing peripheral depth to keep secondary tasks present but non-intrusive.

04

60 FOV constraint

We chose Optimized Density Over Ultra-wide Clutter

Optimizing information density for current hardware by focusing on the "Active Window." We designed for the 60-degree Field of View to prevent neck fatigue and ensure all critical UI is always within the primary ocular sweet spot.

05

Accessibility toggle

We chose Adaptive Input Over Fixed Modalities

Universal design from the start. We integrated high-contrast modes, alternative gesture sets, and assistive haptic cues into the core OS rather than as an afterthought, ensuring Orbit is usable for everyone from day one.
